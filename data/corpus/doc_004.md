# The financial math of a career switch, with worked examples

Most people make this decision on vibes and then reverse-engineer a
justification. Do the arithmetic instead. It rarely gives a clean answer, but
it tells you exactly where the risk lives. Here are three worked examples for a
hypothetical single-primary-earner household: ~$98k salary, a partner earning
~$35k part-time, ~$42k in liquid savings, a $1,750/month mortgage, and roughly
$1,200/month in childcare. Numbers are illustrative; run your own.

## The household's monthly floor

Mortgage $1,750 + childcare $1,200 + everything else (food, utilities,
insurance, car, etc.) — call the realistic bare-bones total **$5,500/month**,
or about **$4,200** if you cut hard (pause childcare, freeze discretionary
spend). We'll use both.

## Scenario A: Full-time bootcamp, quit the job

- Tuition: **$14,000** (paid up front).
- Duration: 6 months with no salary from the switcher.
- Income during: partner's ~$35k/yr ≈ **$2,900/month**.
- Burn at the bare-bones floor: $5,500 − $2,900 = **$2,600/month shortfall**,
  ×6 = **$15,600** out of savings, *plus* the $14k tuition = **~$29,600**.
- Remaining cushion: $42,000 − $29,600 = **~$12,400** — before any job search
  beyond month six.

If the job search runs three extra months (common), add ~$7,800 more burn and
you're near **$4,600** in the bank. That is the danger zone: a single
unexpected expense — a car repair, a medical bill — and you're borrowing. The
bootcamp path is *viable* on these numbers but leaves almost no margin for the
timeline slipping, which it usually does.

## Scenario B: Self-study, keep the job

- Tuition: ~$0 (or a few hundred for courses).
- Income: unchanged. Savings stay intact and even grow.
- Cost: time and ~18-24 months to job-ready, plus burnout risk.
- Financial risk: near zero. Opportunity cost: the salary upside is deferred by
  a year or more, and the plan can quietly stall.

## Scenario C: ISA bootcamp (deferred tuition), part-time or full-time

- Tuition up front: **$0**; instead, e.g., 15% of salary for 24 months once
  earning above a $50k floor, capped at ~1.4× tuition.
- If hired at $80k: 15% ≈ $12,000/year ≈ **$24,000 over two years** (likely
  hitting the cap). You pay *more* than the $14k sticker, but only after you're
  employed, and nothing if you never place.
- This trades up-front cash risk for back-end cost. Attractive when savings are
  thin; expensive if you'd have comfortably paid tuition anyway.

## What the math is really telling you

The bootcamp doesn't fail on tuition; it fails on **runway**. The binding
constraint is how many months the household can survive on one part-time income
while burning savings — and how badly a longer-than-expected job search dents
the cushion. Before choosing the immersive path, the single most important
number to nail down is your true monthly floor and how many months of it you
can absorb. If the answer is "barely six," the plan has no shock absorber, and
a part-time or ISA route may be the more responsible bet — not because it's
braver, but because it doesn't bet the emergency fund on a brochure timeline.
