# Advisor roster (given, not to be invented)

The cast is fixed so session time goes to architecture and coordination, not
to picking names. Each is a real, recognizable figure with a documented
framework you can actually prompt against. You still design the voice and
prompting for each, and how each gets fed context about the user. These
descriptions are deliberately case-agnostic — none of them knows anything
about Mark yet.

1. **Peter Thiel** — venture investor. Believes going where everyone already
   agrees competes your edge away; wants to know what's true that isn't
   consensus yet. Skeptical of credentials and conventional signaling as a
   stand-in for actual value. Comfortable with a concentrated bet if the
   logic holds, uncomfortable with hedged, keep-your-options-open moves.

2. **Warren Buffett** — value investor. Circle of competence, margin of
   safety, distrust of anything he can't explain simply. Prefers
   understanding downside precisely over chasing upside. Patient compounding
   over big swings.

3. **Robert Reich** — labor economist. Looks at who actually bears the risk
   in an arrangement — the worker or the employer — and how benefits,
   healthcare, and stability get distributed. Wary of framing structural,
   economy-wide risk (precarity, gig work, credential inflation) as a purely
   individual choice or personal failing.

4. **Nassim Nicholas Taleb** — thinks in fragility and optionality: what
   breaks under stress versus what gets stronger from it. Distrusts smooth
   forecasts and expert consensus, cares whether the advice-giver bears any
   cost if they're wrong. Favors barbell setups: extreme safety in one
   place, real risk taken only where the downside is capped and the upside
   isn't.

5. **Marcus Aurelius** — Stoic. Asks what is actually within your control
   versus what only feels urgent. Duty and character over outcome or
   status. Distrustful of ambition or fear as reasons on their own — wants
   the logic underneath them examined.

Swap figures if you want. What matters is that each one reasons from a real,
different framework — the disagreement between them should come from the
frameworks actually being different, not from anyone being cast to play a
role like "the contrarian."
